### 0.4 How CKLA writes sounds and spellings [C: K Unit 3 teacher guide; Grade 2 Unit 2]

- A **sound** goes in slashes: /m/. A **spelling** (the letter or letters for a
  sound) goes in single quotes: 'm'. In kindergarten a spelling is called a **sound
  picture** ("You will show students how to make a 'picture' of the sound").
- CKLA's vowel sound names: short **/a/ /e/ /i/ /o/ /u/**; long **/ae/ /ee/ /ie/
  /oe/ /ue/** (as in cake, see, bike, home, cute); two **/oo/** sounds, as in soon
  and as in look (this file tells them apart by key word); **/ou/** (shout),
  **/oi/** (coin), **/aw/** (paw), **/er/ /ar/ /or/**, and **/ə/** (schwa, grade 2).
- **/x/** is one CKLA sound-spelling ('x' as in box), and **/qu/** is one ('qu' as in
  quilt). Do not turn them into /k/ /s/ or /k/ /w/ for a kindergartener.
- There are two /th/ sounds: unvoiced as in **thin/moth** and voiced as in **this**.
  Say "/th/ as in thin" or "/th/ as in this".
- On a page or card, write sounds the CKLA way in your notes, but **say** them to
  the child with a key word: "/ae/, like in cake".

## 1. Keep it fun: class routines that are already games

The fun lives in the wrapper; the moves and words match class. These are real CKLA
routines, so a coach beat built on one is both playful and familiar. Use at most
one per coach.

| CKLA routine | What class does | Game beat (app) |
|---|---|---|
| **Arm blending** [C] | Touch shoulder, elbow, wrist for each sound, then sweep down the arm to say the word. Four or five sounds: add the upper and lower arm. Some classes tap fingers instead [C]. | The buddy taps its shoulder, elbow, wrist as each sound plays; the child swipes down the arm to "zoom" the word. |
| **Chaining** [C] | Change one sound picture at a time to make a new word, real or silly: dad > mad > mat > at > cat > cot > dot. Teacher asks "If that is *nut*, what is this?" and "Did I change the first, middle or last sound?" | Drag one tile to swap a sound; the word morphs. |
| **Mirror, Mirror** [C] | Watch your mouth in a mirror as you say a sound. | "Look how your lips press together for /m/!" with a mouth animation. |
| **Hearing Initial Sounds** [C] | Eyes closed; raise your hand when you hear a word that begins with the sound. | Tap the drum each time a word starts with the sound. |
| **Yellow and green word cards** [C] | Tricky Words go on **yellow** cards ("caution: a part can't be read by blending"); decodable words on **green** ("go ahead and blend"). When a Tricky Word becomes decodable, it "changes color" from yellow to green. | A Tricky Word glows yellow; later in the road it flips to green with a cheer. |
| **Spelling Tree** [C] (grade 1-2) | One tree per sound; a branch per spelling (longer branch = more common); words on leaves; rare spellings go on yellow **"odd duck"** cards. | Sort words onto branches; an odd duck quacks. |
| **Wiggle Cards** [C] (grade 1-2) | Read a short decodable phrase and act it out. | Read "hop on one leg" and the buddy does it. |
| **Silly words** [C] | Chains "sometimes include pseudowords or silly words". | A silly-word round is fine, and true to class. |
| **Think-Pair-Share** [C] | Listen to the question, think, talk to a partner, share. | "Think it, then tell your buddy" before the child answers. |
| **Making Choices** [C] (K-2 vocabulary) | Children move to the "sweet" or "not sweet" side of the room as the teacher names things. | Drag items to two sides. |

## 2. The Reading Road: CKLA's phonics sequence, K → grade 1 → grades 2-3

### 2.1 How to use the steps

- Each step is one CKLA Skills unit, in class order. A word may appear at a step
  only if **every spelling in it has been taught by then**. That is the CKLA rule:
  readers in kindergarten Units 6-7 are "100% decodable", and later readers use only
  taught code plus taught Tricky Words [C: Program Guide]. "No word is inherently
  decodable or non-decodable"; it depends on where the child is in the sequence [C].
- **Structure rules** (CKLA order) [C: K and G1 maps]:
  - Kindergarten Units 3-5: **VC and CVC words only** (no clusters like *st*, *nd*).
  - Clusters start in **K Unit 6** ("consonant clusters of up to five sounds").
  - Two-syllable words: oral in K Units 9-10; reading them is taught in **grade 1
    Unit 4**. Keep printed words one syllable before that.
- **Tricky Words** are words "that cannot be completely decoded using the
  sound-spelling correspondences taught so far" [C: Amplify]. The list at each step
  is cumulative. See 2.8 for the full list, the tricky part of each and when it
  "turns green".
- Every example word below was checked by machine against the code taught up to
  its step, using the CMU pronouncing dictionary
  (`work/coach/tools2/decode2.py`, run `python3 -I decode2.py words.json`; input
  `{"STEP": ["word", ...]}`, step ids as in the headings, e.g. `K-U5`, `G1-U3`,
  `G2-U4-6`, `G3`; it loads the dictionary copy in `work/coach/tools/pylib`). Check
  your own words the same way before you use them. One
  manual override: CKLA uses **dog** as its key word for /g/ with short /o/, so the
  checker accepts it. (This replaces the earlier `work/coach/tools/decodecheck.py`,
  which had an incomplete kindergarten Unit 8 list.)
- Unit lengths are in school days, from Amplify's maps [C].

### 2.2 Before print: kindergarten Units 1-2 (listening only)

**Step 0 · K Unit 1 (10 days)** [C: K map; K Unit 1 guide]
- Awareness of noises (environmental sounds), then of **words in sentences**;
  writing strokes, tripod grip, position words. No letters.
- Game items: "Which sound is a dog barking?", "How many words in 'I see a cat'?"
  (claps), "Point to the top of the picture".

**Step 1 · K Unit 2 (10 days)** [C: K map]
- **Blend syllables** into words, then **blend two and three sounds** into words
  ("orally produce two- and three-sound words"), **identify beginning sounds**,
  left-to-right and top-to-bottom tracking.
- Warm-ups pair syllable blending with hand motions and clapping; arm gestures for
  three sounds start here [P: search summary of the Unit 2 guide].
- Oral blending items used in class (K Unit 3 warm-up) [C]: /m/ /ou/ /s/ → mouse;
  /f/ /i/ /sh/ → fish; /m/ /oo/ /s/ → moose; /ae/ /p/ → ape; /sh/ /ee/ /p/ →
  sheep; /s/ /ee/ /l/ → seal; /f/ /l/ /ie/ → fly; /r/ /a/ /t/ → rat; /k/ /a/ /t/
  → cat; /d/ /o/ /g/ → dog. Oral words may use any sounds; nothing is printed.

### 2.6 Step 20 · Grade 3 (the 3rd grader's year)

Phonics becomes **review of spelling patterns by sound** plus **morphology** [C:

| Unit | Spelling review | Morphology |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Classic Tales | short and long vowels, r-controlled vowels, /er/ spellings, syllables with 'le' | - |
| 2 Animal Classification | suffixes -ed, -ing, -es | prefixes **un-, non-, re-, pre-**; how a prefix changes the meaning of the root word |
| 3 The Human Body | plurals, f→v + -es | prefixes **dis-, mis-** (review un-, non-, re-, pre-) |
| 4 Ancient Rome | r-controlled; /ee/ spellings | suffixes **-er, -or, -ist, -ian, -y, -al** |
| 5 Light and Sound | /ee/ and /ae/ spellings | suffixes **-ous, -ly** |

### 2.7 Grades 4-5: word work and fluency (not Reading Road steps)

- One integrated 90-minute lesson a day: reading, writing, grammar, morphology [C].

## 3. How class solves it: phonics items

Each routine gives what class does (tagged), the coach steps (app), and one worked
coach for a sample item. Kindergarten coaches are spoken; keep each step at or under
18 words (COACH-GUIDE limits: grade 3 at or under 26, grade 5 at or under 32).

**Three rules from CKLA that apply to every phonics coach:**
1. **Say sounds, not letter names**, while blending: "Point to the cards and have
   students say the sounds, not the letter names" [C: K Unit 3]. Letter names are
   taught in K Unit 6; before then, call a letter a **sound picture**.
2. **Never send the child to guess.** CKLA teaches the most common spelling first
   because children "who are not taught in a systematic manner ... develop
   strategies based on guessing. This causes poor decoding" [C: K Unit 3]. No "look
   at the picture and guess the word", no "what would make sense?" for **decoding**.
   (Pictures are fine for **comprehension**; see 4.0.)
3. **Stop sounds need a key word.** CKLA shows children that /t/, /d/ and /k/
   "cannot be stretched" and that "many consonant sounds need to be sounded with
   vowels to be heard distinctly" [C]. In a spoken coach, give a stop sound with its
   key word ("/t/, like the start of tag"); stretch vowels and sounds like /m/, /s/,
   /f/ freely.

### 3.1 First sound (K)

- **Class** [C]: "Hearing Initial Sounds": eyes closed, raise a hand for each word
  that begins with the target sound. "If students have trouble hearing a word's
  initial sound, say the word in a segmented fashion: /m/ . . . /a/ . . . /d/. Then
  repeat the word in its blended form: mad."
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word slowly, sound by sound. (2) Listen to the very first
  sound (hum or hiss it if it stretches; key word if it is a stop). (3) "Now" say each
  choice slowly and find the one with the same first sound.
- **Example**: "Which one starts like 🗺️ map?" (🌙 moon, 🐱 cat, 🐶 dog, ☀️ sun)
  - name: "First Sound"
  - "Say map slowly, like a robot: mmm . . . a . . . p."
  - "Your lips start closed and hum: mmm. That is the first sound."
  - "Now say each picture slowly. Which one starts with that same hum?"

### 3.2 Last sound (K)

- **Class**: identifying beginning and end sounds starts in K Unit 2 [P: scope and
  sequence summary]; the same segment-then-blend support applies [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word in pieces. (2) Stop on the last piece; name it with a key
  word if it is a stop sound. (3) "Now" check the end of each choice.
- **Example**: "Which word ends like 🐱 cat?" (bus, pig, hat, fan)
  - "Say cat in pieces: c . . . a . . . t."
  - "The last piece is the sound that starts 🏷️ tag."
  - "Now say each word in pieces. Which one ends with that sound too?"

### 3.3 Middle sound (K, grade 1)

- **Class** [C]: in a CVC word "the vowel sound will always be the middle sound";
  children hold up the picture card for /i/ or /a/. If stuck, "say the word in a
  segmented fashion: /b/ . . . /i/ . . . /g/". Vowels "are made with the mouth open",
  "are the parts of words you stretch out when you sing", and "every word has a
  vowel sound".
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word slowly and stretch the middle. (2) The middle is the
  vowel: sing it. (3) "Now" stretch the middle of each choice and match.
- **Example**: "Which has the same middle sound as 🐷 pig?" (cat, sit, mop, bed)
  - "Say pig slowly: p . . . iiiii . . . g. Stretch the middle."
  - "That middle sound is a vowel. Sing it: iiiii."
  - "Now stretch the middle of each word. Which one sings iiiii too?"

### 3.4 Rhyme (K)

- **Class** [C: K Domain 1 anthology]: "words that have different beginning sounds
  but end with the same sounds are said to rhyme. For example, cat/hat, big/pig,
  coat/goat, bake/take." Nursery rhymes in Domain 1; making rhyming words in Skills
  Unit 6.
- **Coach**: (1) Rhyming words end the same. (2) Say the word without its first
  sound. (3) "Now" do the same to each choice; find the matching end.
- **Example**: "Which word rhymes with cat?" (hat, cup, dog, sun)
  - "Rhyming words have the same ending sounds."
  - "Say cat, then hide the first sound: -at."
  - "Now say each word and hide its first sound. Which one ends -at?"

### 3.5 Blend sounds you hear (K oral blending)

- **Class** [C: K Unit 3]: "Explain that you will say sounds and you want students
  to blend the sounds into words." Arm gestures: point to the shoulder, the elbow
  and the wrist for three sounds, then sweep along the arm; for two sounds, shoulder,
  elbow, sweep; for four or five, add the upper and lower arm. Some classes tap
  fingers instead. Children who struggle use **cubes on a mat** to "feel" and see the
  sounds.
- **Coach**: (1) Touch a body point for each sound as it plays. (2) "Now" sweep down
  your arm and say them fast. Which word or picture is it?
- **Example**: "/f/ /i/ /sh/. Which picture?" (🐟 fish, 🦊 fox, 🦶 foot, 🍕 pizza)
  - name: "Arm Blend"
  - "Touch your shoulder: /f/. Your elbow: /i/. Your wrist: /sh/."
  - "Now sweep your hand down your arm and say them fast. Which picture is it?"

### 3.6 Segment: say each sound, count the sounds (K Unit 4 on, grade 1)

- **Class** [C: G1 Unit 3 dictation]: "For each word that you say, hold up one finger
  for each sound. Ask students to count the sounds and then draw a line on their paper
  for each sound that they hear." A digraph is one sound: in *broom*, four lines.
  [P: a K Unit 7 classroom video shows students raising a finger for each sound.]
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word slowly; one finger per sound. (2) Remind: two letters
  can be one sound (digraph, 'ee', 'oo'). (3) "Now" count your fingers.
- **Example**: "How many sounds in ship?" (2, 3, 4, 5)
  - "Say ship slowly. Put up one finger for each sound you hear."
  - "Remember: 'sh' is two letters but just one sound."
  - "Now count your fingers. How many sounds?"

### 3.7 Read a word (decode)

- **Class** [C]: Teacher Chaining has a child read a word "first in a segmented
  fashion and then blended". Children say sounds, not letter names. Digraphs are
  bold, and the teacher underlines or circles them so children "treat them as single
  graphemes". New spellings are bold in the reader.
- **Separated digraphs** [C: G2 Unit 2]: the reader must "begin scanning to the right
  and then glancing back again to the left". The teacher draws a **horseshoe** linking
  the vowel and the 'e', and points with a **"V for victory"**: one finger on the vowel,
  one on the 'e'. CKLA's term is **separated digraph** ("split digraph" is also
  fine); it prefers **magic 'e'** over "silent 'e'" and asks teachers not to teach
  "silent letters" at all ("in truth, all letters are silent"; *igh* is "a
  three-letter spelling").
- **Coach**: (1) Point under each sound picture and say its sound (a digraph gets one
  sound; a separated digraph gets the V). (2) "Now" blend the sounds fast. Which
  word or picture matches?
- **Example A (K Unit 4)**: "Read: pen. Which picture?" (🐷, 🖊️, 🐔, 🍳)
  - name: "Sound by Sound"
  - "Point under each sound picture. Say its sound, not its name."
  - "Now blend the three sounds together fast. Which picture matches?"
- **Example B (grade 1, separated digraph)**: "Read: smile. Which picture?"
  - name: "Separated Digraph"
  - "See the 'e' at the end? It teams up with 'i'. Make a V: one finger on each."
  - "That team stands for /ie/, like in bike. 'sm' and 'l' are sounds you know."
  - "Now read it sound by sound and blend. Which picture is it?"

### 3.8 Spell a word (encode)

- **Class** [C: G1 Unit 3 dictation]: (1) hold up a finger for each sound; (2) draw a
  line for each sound; (3) "remind them that the sound /oo/ is spelled with two
  letters"; (4) write a spelling on each line (*b r oo m*); (5) "read the word back";
  (6) check with the **Individual Code Chart**. Weekly spelling lists in grades 1-2
  "only use the spellings they know, so they can segment and blend each word".
  Children may write plausible spellings for code not yet taught ("hunee for honey"),
  but "should be held accountable for spellings that have been taught".
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word; a finger and a line per sound. (2) Write a sound picture
  on each line; a digraph fills one line. (3) "Now" read it back. Does it say the word?
- **Example**: "Spell shop."
  - name: "One Line Per Sound"
  - "Say shop slowly. Put up a finger for each sound, then draw a line for each."
  - "The first sound, /sh/, needs two letters on one line."
  - "Now fill the other lines and read it back. Does it say shop?"
- **Writer rule**: only test spellings already taught at the child's step. Do not mark
  a plausible spelling of untaught code as a "mistake" in a coach.

### 3.9 Tricky Words

- **Class** [C: G1 Unit 3 and G2 Unit 1 guides; K Unit 3 appendix; Amplify]:
  - "Most English words play by the rules ... Words that do not play by the rules ...
    are called 'Tricky Words.'"
  - The teacher writes the word, asks how they "would pronounce it by blending", says
    how we really say it and uses it in a sentence, **circles** the parts "pronounced
    just as one would expect" and **underlines the tricky parts**. Example: "Expected:
    the letters 'th' are pronounced /th/. Tricky: the letter 'e' is pronounced as a
    schwa or as /ee/."
  - Tricky Words go on **yellow** cards (caution) and decodable words on **green**
    ("green light"). In readers "the tricky parts of Tricky Words are underlined to
    help us remember to be careful". When the code for a word is taught, it moves to
    the decodable wall: "a word 'change color' from yellow to green".
  - Then a quick flash-card review: "simply ask students to read the word".
  - [P: NYSED's version of the routine also has students repeat the word and stretch
    its sounds with a finger or arm tap.]
- **Coach**: (1) "This is a Tricky Word. One part doesn't play by the rules." (2) Read
  the expected part. (3) Point to the underlined tricky part and say how it sounds in
  this word. (4) "Now" read the whole word (or find it in the sentence).
- **Example (K Unit 9 on)**: "Read: Sam said, 'I can hop.' What can Sam do?"
  (hop, run, swim, sit)
  - name: "Tricky Word"
  - "'said' is a Tricky Word. The 's' and 'd' play by the rules."
  - "The tricky part is 'ai'. In said, it sounds like /e/, like in pen."
  - "Now read the whole sentence again. What can Sam do?"
- **Never** call it a "sight word" with the child: CKLA uses "Tricky Word", and a
  word stops being tricky once its code is taught [C]. "Sight word" is a different
  idea (any common word read on sight).

### 3.10 Chaining: which sound changed?

- **Class** [C: K Unit 3; G1 Unit 3]: rule 1, "only one spelling changes at a time";
  rule 2, three changes are allowed: **add** a sound picture (ad > mad), **take one
  away** (mad > ad), **swap one** (mat > cat). "If that is nut, what is this?" ...
  "Ask them whether you changed the first, middle, or last sound."
- **Coach**: (1) Read the first word sound by sound. (2) Read the new word sound by
  sound. (3) "Now" compare: first, middle or last?
- **Example**: "cat → cot. Which sound changed?" (first, middle, last, none)
  - name: "Chain It"
  - "Read cat sound by sound. Then read cot sound by sound."
  - "Put cot right under cat, sound picture under sound picture."
  - "Now find the sound picture that changed. Is it first, middle or last?"
- Do not say which sounds stayed the same; with three positions that names the
  answer.

### 3.11 Syllables and two-syllable words (K Unit 2 orally; grade 1 Unit 4 on)

- **Class** [C]: K Unit 2 blends syllables into words (with hand motions and
  claps [P]); K Unit 10 divides syllables orally; grade 1 Unit 4 teaches "the concept
  of a syllable" and to "divide multi-syllable words into parts"; grade 2 Unit 5
  teaches **chunking** to read multisyllable words; grades 2-3 name syllable types
  (closed, magic-e, vowel digraph, r-controlled, open, consonant-le); grades 4-5 sound
  out "syllable by syllable" [C].
- **Coach** (grade 1-2): (1) Find the vowel sounds; every syllable has one. (2) Read
  the first chunk, then the next. (3) "Now" put the chunks together.
- **Example (grade 1)**: "Read: napkin."
  - name: "Chunk It"
  - "Find the two vowels, 'a' and 'i'. Each chunk gets one."
  - "Read the first chunk: nap. Then the second chunk."
  - "Now put the two chunks together fast. What's the word?"
- How to split a word between consonants is not confirmed as a CKLA rule at grade 1
  [U]. Let the app show the chunk break rather than teaching a rule.

### 3.12 Which spelling? (spelling alternatives, grade 1 Unit 5 on)

- **Class** [C: G1 Unit 7; G2 Unit 3]: the teacher shows the sound's **Spelling Card**
  with its **power bar** (how often each spelling is used), outlines vowel spellings
  in **green** and consonant spellings in **red** on the Individual Code Chart, and
  builds a **Spelling Tree**. Position hints: "'ai' is generally used in initial
  position (aim, aid) or medial position (pail ...)"; "'ay' is generally used in final
  position"; for /oe/ "at the end of a word, 'o', 'ow', and 'oe' are used (hippo,
  snow, toe)".
- **Coach**: (1) Say the word; where is the sound (start, middle, end)? (2) Recall which
  spellings of that sound like that spot, using a key word that is **not** one of
  the choices. (3) "Now" pick the spelling that fits.
- **Example**: "Which spelling finishes tr__n (train)?" ('ai', 'ay', 'a_e', 'ee')
  - name: "Pick the Spelling"
  - "Say train. Is the /ae/ sound at the start, middle or end?"
  - "Look at the /ae/ Spelling Tree. Each /ae/ spelling likes certain spots in a word."
  - "Now pick the /ae/ spelling that fits the spot where /ae/ sits in train."
- Writer care: a position hint can narrow the choices to one. If it does, stop after
  step 1 and let the child try with the Spelling Tree picture on screen.

### 3.13 Word parts (grades 3-5 morphology)

- **Class** [C: G3 map; Program Guide]: children "discuss how adding prefixes changes
  the meaning of root words and how the parts of speech of words may change". The
  Program Guide's classroom poster: "-s there is more than one", "-re do again
  (refill)", "-un not (unhappy)", "-pre before (preview)", "-ful full of", "-less
  without", "-ed something has already happened". Grades 4-5 add Greek and Latin
  **roots** (graph, rupt, port, bio, tract, vac, serv, mem).
- **Coach**: (1) Find the root word (or root). (2) Find the prefix or suffix and recall
  what it means. (3) "Now" put the meanings together and match a choice.
- **Example (grade 3)**: "What does *reread* mean?"
  - name: "Word Parts"
  - "Cover the beginning. The root word left is read."
  - "The prefix is re-. Think of refill and retell. What does re- add?"
  - "Now put re- and read together. Which choice matches?"
- If a choice repeats the prefix meaning word for word, stop at "what does re- add?"
  so you do not narrow it to one.

### 3.14 Long words (grades 4-5)

- **Class** [C]: "sound out unfamiliar words syllable by syllable"; spot the schwa
  syllable (*fatal*: the second syllable is pronounced /təl/ but spelled 'tal');
  use morphology to break words apart; "logical, defensible spellings based on code
  knowledge rather than guessing".
- **Coach**: (1) Split off any prefix and suffix. (2) Read the rest syllable by syllable;
  expect a schwa in an unstressed syllable. (3) "Now" read the whole word and check it
  is a word you know.

## 4. How class solves it: comprehension items

### 4.0 What class does after every read-aloud and reader [C unless marked]

**Kindergarten to grade 2, read-alouds (Knowledge strand)** [C: K and G1 Domain 1
anthologies; Program Guide]:
- Before: review "What Have We Already Learned?", preview vocabulary, and give a
  **Purpose for Listening** ("listen carefully to find out exactly what things are
  red ..."). In grade 1, children **predict**; afterwards: "Were your predictions ...
  correct? Why or why not?"
- After: **Comprehension Questions**, each labelled **Literal, Inferential or
  Evaluative** in the teacher guide. Kindergarten "focus[es] mostly on literal
  understanding"; "by Grade 2, inferential and evaluative questions are core".
- The support line printed before every question set: "**If students have difficulty
  responding to questions, reread pertinent passages of the read-aloud and/or refer to
  specific images.** If students give one-word answers ... acknowledge correct
  responses by expanding students' responses using richer and more complex language.
  **Ask students to answer in complete sentences by having them restate the question
  in their responses.**"
- Inference is modelled with "How do you know?" (K: "Who can find the roses in the
  picture? How do you know they are roses? (They're red.)").
- **Think-Pair-Share**, said to the children: "I am going to ask you a question. I will
  give you a minute to think about the question, and then I will ask you to turn to
  your neighbor and discuss the question. Finally, I will call on several of you to
  share what you discussed with your partner."
- Teacher prompts that send children back to the text: "What in the Read-Aloud text
  made you ask that question?", "**Can you think of a detail in the Read-Aloud text that
  might help answer that question?**", "**What evidence does the text provide that is
  helpful here?**" [C: Program Guide].
- Story words taught in grade 1: **characters** ("the people or animals in a story"),
  **setting** ("where it takes place"), **plot** ("the beginning, middle, and end
  events"), **moral** ("a lesson, about how to behave"), **fiction** [C].
- Retelling and summarising: **Somebody Wanted But So Then** chart [C: G1 map
  Domains 1-4; G1 Domain 1; G2 map Domain 2].
- Vocabulary: **Word Work** after each read-aloud, in six moves [C]: (1) "In the
  nursery rhyme you heard, 'Sugar is sweet.'" (2) "Say the word *sweet* with me."
  (3) a kid-friendly meaning, (4) an example, (5) children use the word in a sentence,
  (6) "What's the word we've been talking about?" Then often a **Making Choices** game.

**Kindergarten to grade 2, decodable readers (Skills strand)** [C: K map; G1 Unit 3 guide]:
- From K Unit 6, children "demonstrate comprehension by answering story questions";
  from K Unit 9, by "drawing/writing"; in K Unit 7 "students are encouraged and
  expected to answer in complete sentences".
- Grade 1: "Each story is accompanied by a set of discussion questions ... focusing
  on literal, inferential, and evaluative question types." The teacher reads the
  questions aloud; children answer orally "in complete sentences". "**When answering
  the question, ask students to cite the part of the story that guided their
  answer.**" Worksheets have "a space for students to mark the **page number** where
  they found the answer". "**Remind students to look back at the story to find the
  answers.**"

**Grades 3-5** [C: Program Guide; G3-G5 maps]:
- Questions are text-dependent and of three kinds. **Literal**: recall key details;
  students "paraphrase and/or refer back to the portion of the text where the specific
  answer is provided". **Inferential**: "infer information from the text and ... think
  critically"; students "refer back to the portions of the text that lead to and support
  the inference". **Evaluative**: "form an opinion or make a judgment", referring back to
  "the portion(s) of the text that substantiate" it, for example comparing points of
  view, first- and secondhand accounts, how reasons support points, two texts on one
  topic.
- **Close reading** lessons (from grade 2 Unit 2): questions tagged VOC (vocabulary),
  SYN (syntax: complex sentences), COMP (understanding; "students may provide multiple
  responses using different pieces of evidence, grounding inferences logically in the
  text") and LIT (literary devices). "Have all students provide a written response
  before one student responds orally."
- Grade 3 unit skills include point of view, plot and sequence, themes (Unit 1);
  **main idea** (Unit 4); "Identify conjunction **because** as the signal of an
  answer to a **why** question" (Unit 6); **cause and effect** and compare and contrast
  (Unit 7); "use sentence context to determine word meanings and then consult a
  dictionary" (Unit 9); K-W-L charts (Unit 3). Grade 4: "cause and effect and ability
  to make text-based inferences" (Unit 1); opinion "with evidence to support" (Unit 2).
  Grade 5: "literal comprehension and text-based inferencing" (Unit 1); "gist,
  explanation, details, and inferences" (Unit 5); "main argument and evidence" (Unit 8).
- Grade 3 discussion questions are presumably labelled the same way [U: confirmed for
  K-2 and 4-5 only].

**The coach pattern for every comprehension item (app, built from the [C] moves above):**
1. **Look back**: point to where in the passage (or picture, for K) to look. This is
   CKLA's "reread pertinent passages ... refer to specific images" and "look back at the
   story".
2. **Ask yourself**: the question in text terms, or the clue to notice ("What did she
   do right after ...?", "How do you know?").
3. **"Now"**: hand the last move to the child ("Now pick the choice that matches what
   the story says").
For two-part (evidence) items: answer Part A from the text, then "find the sentence
that proves it", CKLA's "cite the part of the story that guided their answer". This is
also the shape of the composite items on Maryland's new MSTAR test (see
CURRICULUM-NOTES.md).

### 4.1 Who, what, where (literal)

- Class: Literal questions; "Who are the characters ...?", "What is the setting ...?
  In other words, where does the story take place?" [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Find the sentence that names who or where. (2) "Now" read it again and
  pick the choice it matches.
- **Example (K, read aloud)**: "Where does the boy watch his sheep?"
  - name: "Look Back"
  - "Let's listen to the start of the story again." (replay the first sentences)
  - "Listen for a place word. Where is the boy?"
  - "Now pick the picture that shows that place."

### 4.2 Sequence (first, next, last)

- Class: plot is "the beginning, middle, and end events" [C]; grade 1 retellings use
  "temporal words to signal event order" [C]; grade 3 Unit 1 "plot and sequence" [C].
  Exact sequence words CKLA prints for children (first, next, then, last) were not seen
  [U]; they are standard.
- **Coach**: (1) Find the event named in the question in the passage. (2) Look just
  before or just after it. (3) "Now" pick the event that comes there.
- For order puzzles: "Find what happened FIRST. Then ask: what happened because of that,
  or right after it?"

### 4.3 Main idea and key details

- Class: grade 1 Knowledge domains practise "identify important information in a text"
  [C]; grade 3 Unit 4 "main idea and key vocabulary" [C]; grade 5 uses the word
  **gist** [C]. CKLA's exact main-idea routine wording was not seen [U].
- **Coach** (app): (1) Say what each part (paragraph) is about in a few words. (2) What
  do all the parts tell about? (3) "Now" pick the choice that covers the whole passage,
  not just one part.

### 4.4 Character feelings and traits

- Class: literal "How does he feel? (lonely)" when the text says it; inferential when it
  does not, checked with "How do you know?" [C: G1 and K Domain 1]. Grade 3 Unit 1
  "analyze characters"; grade 4 Unit 6 "inferring character" [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Find what the character says or does at that moment. (2) Ask: how would
  someone feel who says or does that? What words show it? (3) "Now" pick the feeling the
  clues point to.

### 4.5 Cause and effect (why did it happen?)

- Class: grade 3 Unit 6 teaches **because** as "the signal of an answer to a why
  question"; grade 3 Unit 7 "identify cause and effect"; grade 4 cause-and-effect essays
  [C]. Inferential question in grade 1: "What does the boy decide to do because he is
  lonely?" [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Find the event the question asks about. (2) Look for **because**, **so**,
  or what happened just before it. (3) "Now" pick the reason the text gives.

### 4.6 Inference

- Class: Inferential questions; "How do you know?"; students "refer back to the
  portions of the text that lead to and support the inference" [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Find the clues in the text (what it says). (2) Add what you already know.
  (3) "Now" pick the choice the clues support, and point to the clue.

### 4.7 Vocabulary from context

- Class [C: Program Guide]: "Context clues, inflections, and morphological clues can be
  used to help students decipher these words." Prompts: "**What is happening in this
  text? What happened before the word was used? What happened after the word was
  used?**" Other clues: root words and affixes, **contrast** words like "unlike",
  **cause and effect**, **definitions** in the text. Grade 3 Unit 9: use sentence context,
  then check a dictionary [C].
- **Coach**: (1) Read the sentence with the word, and the one before. (2) Ask what is
  happening, and what the word must mean to fit. (Grades 3-5: also check for a prefix,
  suffix or root.) (3) "Now" try each choice in place of the word. Which one makes sense?
- Name: "Context Clues" (CKLA's term). "Clue words" is not a CKLA term [U]; prefer
  "context clues".

### 4.8 Text evidence (two-part questions)

- Class: grade 1, "cite the part of the story that guided their answer" and mark the
  page number [C]; grades 4-5, students "find evidence and use it appropriately" and
  "cite evidence for their opinions and answers" [C: Program Guide].
- **Coach**: (1) Answer Part A by looking back at the passage. (2) For Part B, find the
  sentence that shows your Part A answer is true. (3) "Now" pick the sentence that
  proves it best.

### 4.9 Retell and summarise

- Class: **Somebody Wanted But So Then** (grades 1-2) [C]; beginning, middle, end [C].
- **Coach (grades 1-2)**: (1) Somebody: who is the story about? (2) Wanted ... But ...: what
  did they want, and what got in the way? (3) "Now" So ... Then: how did it end? Pick the
  summary that tells all of that.

### 4.10 Moral, lesson, theme

- Class: fables "teach a moral, or a lesson, about how to behave"; children may
  paraphrase ("Don't lie.") [C]. Grade 3 "identify themes" such as friendship,
  hospitality, responsibility [C].
- **Coach**: (1) What mistake or good choice did the main character make? (2) What happened
  because of it? (3) "Now" pick the lesson that warns about that mistake or praises
  that choice.

### 4.11 Compare and contrast (evaluative)

- Class: "How are roses and violets alike? How are they different?" (K evaluative);
  "How do King Log and King Crane act differently?" (grade 1); grade 3 Unit 7 and
  grade 5 Unit 2 compare and contrast [C].
- **Coach**: (1) What does the text say about the first one? (2) And the second? (3) "Now"
  pick the choice that is true of both (or of only one, as the question asks).

### 4.12 Prediction

- Class: Purpose for Listening, predictions before and during the read-aloud, then
  "Were your predictions ... correct? Why or why not?" [C].
- **Coach**: (1) What has happened so far? (2) What would a character like this do next?
  (3) "Now" pick the most likely next event, and be ready to say which clue told you.

## 5. Words to use and words to avoid, by grade

"Use" words are CKLA's own [C] unless marked. Teacher-only labels (Literal,
Inferential, Evaluative, Basic Code, Advanced Code, VOC/SYN/COMP/LIT) are never said to
the child (app).

| Grade | Use with the child | Avoid with the child |
|---|---|---|
| **K** | sound; **sound picture** (before letter names in Unit 6), then letter; blend; "say it slowly"; first, middle, last sound; vowel ("the part you can sing"); rhyme; **Tricky Word**; "tricky part"; "plays by the rules"; digraph (Unit 7); "two letters, one sound"; long and short vowels (Unit 10); magic 'e' or "the 'e' teams up"; uppercase and lowercase; characters (with help) | letter names while blending before Unit 6; "sight word"; "silent e" and "silent letter"; "guess", "look at the picture to guess the word", "what makes sense?" for decoding; /ks/ for 'x' and /kw/ for 'qu'; isolated stop sounds without a key word; printed words with untaught code |
| **1** | all K words; spelling; **Tricky Spelling** (a spelling with more than one sound); **spelling alternative** (another way to spell the same sound); separated digraph; syllable, chunk; Spelling Tree; odd duck; Individual Code Chart; "look back at the story"; "what part of the story told you?"; characters, setting, plot, moral; Somebody Wanted But So Then | "sight word"; "silent e"; "vowel team rule" chants (for example "when two vowels go walking") [U: not a CKLA term, and wrong for many words]; spelling items that test untaught spellings |
| **2** | all grade 1 words; **schwa**; vowel team [C: grade 2 objective]; chunking; syllable types (as names only, if at all); contractions; synonyms, antonyms; close reading; evidence; "restate the question" | "silent letters" ("all letters are silent"; *igh* is one spelling); invented sound nicknames [U] |
| **3** | prefix, suffix, **root word**; part of speech; context clues; dictionary, **guide words**; homograph; main idea; point of view; cause and effect; "because answers why"; theme; text evidence; "sound it out" (fine) | "sight word"; "guess the word"; unit or lesson numbers |
| **4-5** | syllable by syllable; schwa; roots (graph, port, tract ...); evidence, "cite", "support your answer"; inference; gist (grade 5); main argument; firsthand and secondhand account; point of view; figurative language | lesson or unit numbers; teacher labels (Literal, Inferential, Evaluative) |

Kid-voice phrasings that stay true to class (app): "Let's look back at the story",
"Which part told you?", "Say it in a whole sentence: The boy felt ... because ...",
"That word plays by the rules, so we can blend it", "This one's a Tricky Word.
Careful, like a yellow light."
