Pass dated 2026-10-22, for a first grader in Anne Arundel County Public Schools (AACPS), Maryland.

## Method and limits

- **No primary document was opened.** `curl` got a 403 CONNECT rejection from the egress proxy for nextgenscience.org, education.alaska.gov, marylandpublicschools.org and aacps.org. WebFetch returned `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND` for nextgenscience.org. The non-Maryland hosts the brief suggested did not work either.
- All evidence comes from WebSearch result summaries, which quote or paraphrase the documents. Each source is labelled primary (MSDE, AACPS, NGSS, another state DOE) or secondary (IXL, Common Planner, aggregators, news, other districts).
- About 40 searches were used, over the budget of about 25. The extra searches went to the Grade 1 social studies indicators, which are only reachable through fragments.
- Verdict scale (the same as the earlier reports):
  - **confirmed**: two or more independent sources agree, at least one of them primary, and nothing contradicts them.
  - **partly**: the core holds, but the details are thin, secondary or in conflict.
  - **unverified**: one weak source, or none.
  - **refuted**: the evidence contradicts the claim.

## Headlines for pack writers

1. **Grade 1 NGSS is small and sharply bounded: 9 performance expectations plus 3 K-2 engineering ones.** Only three topics:
   - light and sound;
   - plant and animal parts, parent and offspring behaviour, and "like but not exactly like" parents;
   - sky patterns and daylight across the seasons.

   Weather, plant needs, habitats and matter belong to kindergarten or grade 2 NGSS. Keep them out of grade 1 *science* packs.
2. **The assessment boundaries matter for question writing:**
   - no speed of light;
   - no inheritance mechanisms, metamorphosis or hybrids in the traits standard;
   - daylight compared only in relative terms ("more or less daylight in winter"), never in hours;
   - star patterns limited to "seen at night, not during the day".
3. **Maryland Grade 1 social studies centres on the school community.** It has four units in the order Civics, Geography, Economics, History. The phrases found are:
   - "How can I be a responsible and engaged citizen?";
   - "What makes my school community special?";
   - goods and services "offered by a school and local businesses";
   - "How is our school different today than in the past?"

   The June 2025 revision made only small changes to PreK-2: about 4 of 16 units, including adding "responsibilities" to a Grade 1 civics objective.
4. **No AACPS Grade 1 health scope was found.** Maryland's revised Health Framework (adopted 24 Feb 2026, full implementation 2027-28) has six content standards, 1a-1f. Keep health packs general: feelings, germs and handwashing, healthy food, teeth, everyday safety and medicine safety.
5. **AACPS specifics found for elementary, grade 1 included:**
   - every elementary student gets **one hour a week of "EEE" (Enhancing Elementary Excellence) STEM**, built on the Engineering Design Process and NGSS;
   - current K-3 science is described as "90 days with 30-minute modules";
   - a new K-5 science program is being procured for 2027-28.

   **No new Grade 1 program for 2026-27 was found, and no countywide Grade 1 Arlington Echo trip.**
6. **Overlap with reading.** CKLA Grade 1 already carries The Human Body, Astronomy and Animals and Habitats (see `research-ela.md`). Science packs should take the NGSS angle so they do not repeat the reading packs:
   - sky *patterns* and daylight, not planets and constellations;
   - animal *parts and parent behaviour*, not habitats.

## A. Science

### A1. Grade 1 waves: light and sound (1-PS4-1 to 1-PS4-4)

**Verdict: confirmed.** Several independent copies agree, including other states' DOEs. Quoted or closely paraphrased in the results:

| PE | Statement |
|---|---|
| **1-PS4-1** | "Plan and conduct investigations to provide evidence that vibrating materials can make sound and that sound can make materials vibrate." |
| **1-PS4-2** | "Make observations to construct an evidence-based account that objects [in darkness] can be seen only when illuminated." |
| **1-PS4-3** | "Plan and conduct investigations to determine the effect of placing objects made with different materials in the path of a beam of light." |
| **1-PS4-4** | "Use tools and materials to design and build a device that uses light or sound to solve the problem of communicating over a distance." |

Clarification statements and boundaries:

- **1-PS4-1.**
  - "Examples of vibrating materials that make sound could include tuning forks and plucking a stretched string."
  - Sound making matter vibrate: "holding a piece of paper near a speaker".
- **1-PS4-2.** The search summary dropped "in darkness". The full NGSS text, recalled and not quoted this pass, reads "objects in darkness can be seen only when illuminated". Its clarification cites a dark room, a pinhole box and a cave explorer with a flashlight. Illumination can come from an outside light source or from an object that gives off its own light.
- **1-PS4-3.**
  - Clarification (recalled): materials that are transparent (clear plastic), translucent (wax paper), opaque (cardboard) and reflective (mirror).
  - Assessment boundary (recalled): no speed of light.
- **1-PS4-4.**
  - "Examples of devices could include a light source to send signals, paper cup and string 'telephones,' and a pattern of drum beats."
  - Assessment boundary (recalled): no technological details of how communication devices work.
- **Crosscutting concept:** cause and effect. "Simple tests can be designed to gather evidence to support or refute student ideas about causes."

Sources:
- https://www.cpp.edu/respect/resources/documents_1st/sound/gr1.sound_ngss.pdf
- https://natomasunified.org/isc/wp-content/uploads/sites/22/2015/10/waves-Light-and-Sound-ngss.pdf
- https://education.alaska.gov/standards/science/1st%20Grade Science.pdf (Alaska's adaptation)
- https://navigator.fcps.net/storage/1416/1st-Grade-Science-Standards.pdf

**For writers.** Questions can safely use these terms:
- vibration;
- loud and soft;
- the four material words: see-through (transparent), lets some light through (translucent), blocks light (opaque), bounces light (reflective);
- shadows, as opaque objects blocking light;
- "you need light to see";
- signalling with a flashlight or drum.

Avoid the speed of light, how phones work, and words like "frequency" or "pitch physics".

### A2. Structure and function: 1-LS1-1 (mimicking nature) and 1-LS1-2 (parents and offspring)

**Verdict: confirmed.** The sources are California's CA NGSS pages (verbatim NGSS), The Wonder of Science and a UCSC teaching guide.

**1-LS1-1.** "Use materials to design a solution to a human problem by mimicking how plants and/or animals use their external parts to help them survive, grow, and meet their needs."
- Quoted clarification: "designing clothing or equipment to protect bicyclists by mimicking turtle shells, acorn shells, and animal scales".
- The rest of the clarification (recalled, consistent with the summary's "detection devices modeled on eyes and ears"):
  - stabilising structures by mimicking animal tails and plant roots;
  - keeping out intruders by mimicking thorns and quills;
  - detecting intruders by mimicking eyes and ears.
- The core ideas are LS1.A (structure and function) and LS1.D (information processing).

**1-LS1-2.** "Read texts and use media to determine patterns in behavior of parents and offspring that help offspring survive." Clarification: "the signals that offspring make (such as crying, cheeping, and other vocalizations) and the responses of the parents (such as feeding, comforting, and protecting the offspring)."

### A3. Inherited traits: 1-LS3-1

**Verdict: confirmed.**
- **Statement:** "Make observations to construct an evidence-based account that young plants and animals are like, but not exactly like, their parents."
- **Clarification:** "leaves from the same kind of plant are the same shape but can differ in size; and, a particular breed of dog looks like its parents but is not exactly the same."
- **Assessment boundary:** "Assessment does not include inheritance or animals that undergo metamorphosis or hybrids."

Sources:
- https://thewonderofscience.com/1ls31
- https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/science?c0=18%2C24
- https://www.cde.ca.gov/CI/pl/documents/cangssgr1-dci.pdf

**For writers.**
- Use puppies, kittens, ducklings and seedlings.
- Avoid caterpillar-to-butterfly or tadpole-to-frog as "looks like its parent" items, because a young caterpillar does not resemble a butterfly.
- Avoid mules and other hybrids, genes and DNA.

### A4. Sun, moon and stars, and daylight across seasons: 1-ESS1-1 and 1-ESS1-2

**Verdict: confirmed.** The sources are the NGSS site (by title), AMNH, the CA CDE and The Wonder of Science, with "the same wording" across all of them.

**1-ESS1-1.** "Use observations of the sun, moon, and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted."
- Boundary: "Assessment of star patterns is limited to stars being seen at night and not during the day."
- Clarification (recalled): the sun and moon appear to rise in one part of the sky, move across the sky, and set; stars other than our sun are visible at night but not during the day.

**1-ESS1-2.** "Make observations at different times of year to relate the amount of daylight to the time of year."
- Clarification: "Emphasis is on relative comparisons of the amount of daylight in the winter to the amount in the spring or fall."
- Boundary: "Assessment is limited to relative amounts of daylight, not quantifying the hours or time of daylight."

Sources:
- https://www.nextgenscience.org/dci-arrangement/1-ess1-earths-place-universe
- https://www.amnh.org/content/download/207901/3087861/file/NGSS-AMNH-CARD-DECK-1-ESS1.pdf
- https://thewonderofscience.com/1ess11
- https://thewonderofscience.com/1ess12
- https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/28983
- https://education.ucsc.edu/ellisa/pdfs/ngss-ca-gr1-dci-ess1_sun_moon_stars.pdf

**For writers.** Safe items:
- the sun rises in the east and sets in the west (a predictable pattern);
- the sun and moon seem to move across the sky;
- we see stars at night, but not in the daytime, because the sun is so bright;
- the sun is a star;
- winter has less daylight than summer, so it gets dark earlier.

**Unverified for scope:** moon phases. They are not in the 1-ESS1-1 text found here; NGSS places the cause of phases in middle school. If the moon "looking different on different nights" is used at all, keep it to observation and do not explain why.
