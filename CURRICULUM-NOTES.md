# What this game is based on, and how confident that is

Written September 2026 and rechecked in October 2026, for the 2026-2027 school year,
for one kindergartener, one third grader and one fifth grader in Anne Arundel County
Public Schools (AACPS), Maryland.

## How the content was made

The questions, explanations, hints, SHOW ME HOW coaching steps and Reading Road
stories were written by an AI assistant at the parent's direction, working from
official curriculum information that is publicly available. They were then checked
by automated tests (shape, keys, decodability, word limits, answer leaks, every pack
dealing a full round) and by further AI review passes that re-solved every question
and were told to assume defects exist. This is a family-made practice game. **It is
not an AACPS product, and AACPS and its teachers have not reviewed or endorsed it.**
Nor is it a product of Amplify or McGraw Hill, the publishers of CKLA and Reveal
Math, and they have not reviewed or endorsed it.

## Confirmed

- **AACPS uses Amplify CKLA for K-5 English language arts** (piloted 2023-24,
  district-wide from 2024-25) **and McGraw Hill Reveal Math for K-5 math.** Both
  are named in AACPS releases through 2025-26; nothing found suggests a change for
  2026-27, so "still current" is a strong inference rather than a quoted fact.
- **The CKLA unit sequences** for each grade (2nd Edition curriculum maps), which the
  reading packs follow, with one exception in grade 5 (below):
  - **Kindergarten** (12 Knowledge domains): Nursery Rhymes and Fables · The Five
    Senses · Stories · Plants · Farms · Native Americans · Kings and Queens ·
    Seasons and Weather · Columbus and the Pilgrims · Colonial Towns · Taking Care
    of the Earth · Presidents and American Symbols. Plus a Skills sequence from
    oral phonological awareness to the long vowel sounds.
  - **Grade 3** (11 units): Classic Tales: The Wind in the Willows · Classification
    of Animals · The Human Body · Ancient Rome · Light and Sound · The Vikings and
    Norse Mythology · Astronomy · Native Americans · European Exploration of North
    America · Colonial America · Ecology.
  - **Grade 5** (9 units): Personal Narratives · Early American Civilizations ·
    Poetry · Don Quixote · Renaissance · Reformation · A Midsummer Night's Dream ·
    Native Americans · Chemical Matter. Amplify's map puts The House on Mango Street
    in grade 4 (Unit 6), so the game's Mango Street pack sits with grade 4, on the 5th
    grader's 4th grade review shelf. There is no separate Chemical Matter reading pack;
    the 5th grade science pack Matter and Its Properties practices the same science.
- **Maryland's revised math standards are real and in effect this year.** The
  "Revised Maryland College and Career Ready Standards for Mathematics" were adopted
  29 July 2025; MSDE's crosswalks read "Implementation: SY 2026-2027" for PreK-8.
  They are no longer Common Core-shaped. Four domains run through elementary:
  **NOS** (Number and Operation Sense), **AT** (Algebraic Thinking), **GR**
  (Geometric Reasoning and Measurement), **DS** (Reasoning with Data and Statistics).
  *(September's notes said these codes were refuted. That was wrong.)* What it
  means for these three children:
  - **Kindergarten:** counting backward from a number within 20 (K.NOS.A.3) and
    repeating patterns (K.AT.B) are **genuinely new** kindergarten expectations —
    both have their own packs. Fluency adding and subtracting within 5 stays. **Making
    10 moves to grade 1** in the June 2025 draft (not rechecked against the adopted
    text), and **subitizing is a PreK standard** — the "Ways to Make 10" and "See How
    Many, Fast" packs are still good kindergarten math, just not new requirements.
  - **Grade 3:** place value now extends to **10,000** (3.NOS.A.1; Common Core
    stopped at 1,000) — the Place Value pack already works to 10,000. Fractions stay
    limited to denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8.
  - **Grade 5:** multi-digit and decimal operations (5.NOS.B), fraction operations
    (5.NOS.C-D), numerical expressions (5.AT.A), volume (5.GR.C), data (5.DS.A).
  No question in the app cites a standard code; every question is written to the
  content, which is why the September mistake broke nothing.
- **MSTAR replaces MCAP this spring for grades 3-8 reading and math.** MSDE:
  "Beginning with the Spring 2027 assessment administration window (March 29-May 28),
  Maryland will transition to MSTAR assessments for English Language Arts and
  Mathematics in grades 3-8." It adapts in stages and is about 25% shorter. Grades
  4-5 reading is 75-90% single-answer multiple choice; the rest is
  **multiple-select, composite (very likely the two-part evidence item) and
  technology-enhanced items**; math is 60-75% multiple choice/multiple select and
  20-35% technology-enhanced, plus short constructed response. **The app's puzzle
  questions — pick all, answer-then-prove, put in order, sort — practice similar
  formats.** The blueprints themselves have not been opened (see "Worth an hour"
  item 1), so the match is not exact.
- **Grade 5 science stays MCAP MISA this year** (MSTAR science arrives 2027-28),
  and **MISA covers grades 3, 4 and 5**: "The grade 5 MISA uses all the Performance
  Expectations from the Maryland NGSS for grades 3 through 5." This is why the fifth
  grader's 4th-grade science packs sit under *Also on the science test*, not review.
- **Maryland adopted NGSS as its science standards** (State Board, 11-0, 25 June 2013).
- **Maryland's social studies frameworks were revised June 2025.** Grade 5 runs from
  founding documents and government through westward expansion, the antebellum
  era, the Civil War, its aftermath ("Aftermath of the Civil War" covers the 13th-15th
  Amendments, Jim Crow, the Freedmen's Bureau and Maryland HBCUs), to 1900-today;
  the exact unit list was not retrieved. Grades 4 *and* 5 use Maryland as "a lens
  into the national story", and grade 3's history unit includes colonial settlement.
- **AACPS 2026-27 calendar** (adopted 3 December 2025): grades 1-5 start Monday
  24 August; all kindergarten students attend from 31 August; marking period 1 ends
  **Friday 23 October**; MP2 ends Friday 15 January 2027; MP3 ends about 25 March
  (one source); last day Friday 11 June 2027 (tentative — shortened if snow days
  are not used).

## SHOW ME HOW: whose methods

The coach walks a stuck child through the method the programs teach, with the
question's own numbers and words. Every written question (10,400+) has its own
steps, written and then reviewed beside its question; math questions generated each
round build theirs from their own numbers; each pack also has its method as a
fallback. Math follows Reveal Math's strategies grade by grade and respects when
each starts: kindergarten counts all (touch and count, the last number tells how
many, ten frames); 1st grade adds make a 10, count on and back, doubles and the open
number line; 2nd grade breaks numbers into tens and ones with no standard algorithm;
3rd grade says "regroup", never "carry" or "borrow"; 4th grade multiplies with the
area model and partial products; 4th and 5th grade divide with partial quotients
(long division is not in Reveal's grade 4 or 5 lessons); the multiplication
algorithm waits for grade 5. Reading follows CKLA's routines: stop sounds always
given with a key word, arm blending, one finger per sound, hide-the-first-sound for
rhymes, Tricky Words (never "sight word" or "silent e"), and Look Back for questions
about a story. Science, social studies and health use a reasoning routine. Lesson
numbers are never used, because they differ between Reveal editions, and how AACPS
maps Reveal onto Maryland's revised standards has not been published; where
Maryland adds something Reveal does not teach, the coach teaches the plainest
standard way.

## 📖 Reading Road: the phonics sequence

The Reading Road follows the order CKLA 2nd Edition teaches the sound-spellings
(the kindergarten and grade 1 Skills units), from the CKLA curriculum maps
and the Core Knowledge teacher guides: listening skills first, then m a t d · o c g
i · n h s f · v z p e · b l r u · w j y x k, then blends, digraphs, double letters,
magic e, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels and spelling alternatives, then longer words
(grades 2-3) and comprehension (grades 4-5). Every story a child is asked to read is
checked word by word, by spelling and by sound (against a pronouncing dictionary), to
use only the sounds and Tricky Words taught by that stop. Limits: exactly which lesson
within a unit introduces each Tricky Word is not verified; the teacher-guide scripts
are the 2013 Core Knowledge edition, whose wording Amplify may have changed.

## Changed after an AI review from the school's point of view (October 2026)

Three AI review passes, one reading as a principal, one as a K-2 literacy and math
specialist and one as a 3-5 instructional coach (Reveal Math, CKLA, MSTAR and the
grade 5 science test), checked the game against CKLA, Reveal Math and this project's
own research. These were AI passes, not people. What changed:

- **What the game says about itself.** The About page now says at the top that the
  game is a family project, not an AACPS product, and that AACPS and its teachers
  have not reviewed or endorsed it. It says plainly that an AI assistant wrote the
  content, and that it was the AI assistant that could not open the official
  websites. The parent dashboard no longer says the reading packs match "the real
  unit sequence" (they follow the 2nd Edition's, and the edition in use is not
  confirmed) or that the puzzles practice MSTAR's formats "exactly". Two packs
  called "MCAP Test Prep" are now "Test-Style Practice", and "Body Systems & Growing
  Up" is now "Body Systems & Healthy Choices".
- **Kindergarten phonics words.** "Sight words" and "silent e" / "quiet e" are gone
  from kindergarten and 1st grade; the game says Tricky Words, magic e and "the e
  teams up", as CKLA does. The pack "K: Sight Words" is now "Little Words to Spell",
  and "Long Vowels & Silent E" is now "Long Vowels & Magic E". Letter teams such as
  oa and igh are one spelling of one sound, not a vowel plus silent letters.
- **Stop sounds.** Explanations and the kindergarten blending walkthrough no longer
  write /b/, /k/, /d/, /t/ and the other stop sounds as "buh", "kuh", "duh", "tuh".
  They use a key word, as the Reading Road already did: "the same quick sound as
  bed", "the quick sound at the start of tag". The pre-K "What sound does B make?"
  items for the stop sounds now ask which word starts with that sound.
- **One limit of read-aloud.** When the game sounds out a word for blending
  ("c... a... t..."), the speech engine still says "kuh, aaa, tuh": no speech engine
  can say a clipped stop sound, and naming a key word for each sound would turn a
  three-sound blend into a listening puzzle. A grown-up can model the clipped sounds.
  The game's About page says this too, under "Limits".
- **Spelling choices** ("noh / nu / nou / no") are read letter by letter, so the four
  choices no longer all sound like "no". Kindergarten and 1st grade answer tiles show
  the number the voice names ("Choice 1"). Where the answers are numbers or pictures to count, the
  tiles have no number, so a numeral is never set beside a group to count, and the
  voice reads the answers instead.
- **Stories attached.** Kindergarten questions whose explanation quotes a class story
  ("The story says...") now show that story, with its "Read me the story" button.
- **Wrong items fixed.** "Car" was taught as three sounds (it is /k/ + /ar/); a pizza
  slice was called a triangle in kindergarten while 1st grade says it is not one;
  "owl" was used to test the letter O at kindergarten; 2 ounces was called "less than
  a tenth of a pound" (it is one eighth); Arlington Echo was said to be a 3rd grade
  trip.
- **Math words.** Grade 3 and the 2nd grade review say "regroup", not "carry" and
  "borrow". Grade 4 and 5 division is taught with partial quotients, not long
  division ("bring down"), and grade 5 decimal division shares by place value
  instead of "divide as if the point were not there, then bring it straight up".
  Grade 5 decimals say "3.5 is the same as 3.50", not "add a zero" or "annex zeros"
  (Maryland's grade 5 companion guide warns against that phrase). The grade 3
  fractions warm-up keeps to denominators 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 and no longer adds
  fractions.
- **The story, not a keyword.** Word-problem explanations no longer give rules such
  as "total means add", "left means subtract" or "together means add"; they say what
  happens in the story ("the boys and the girls together make all the students, so
  add").
- **Made-up school names.** Two made-up school names in story passages were changed,
  because they were too close to the names of real Anne Arundel County schools.
- **Not changed:** grade 5 power-of-10 hints still describe where the decimal point
  moves. That pattern is named in the standard itself, and SHOW ME HOW gives the
  place-value reason ("each digit moves two places").

## How the questions were built and checked

Every bank was written by an AI assistant at the parent's direction, from a topic
brief and a strict format contract, read by a second AI pass told to assume defects
exist, and repaired. AACPS and its teachers have not reviewed the questions. In
October 2026 every question also gained a written **why** (shown after any answer)
and, wherever a second try is offered, a **hint** (for that try), written per child
at that child's reading level — and writing them meant re-solving every question,
which surfaced and fixed a further round of defects: wrong keys, duplicate answer
choices, two defensible answers, and reading questions whose passage was missing or
cut short. Two mechanical gates run over everything: one checks shape (four distinct
options, keys in range, no references to pictures that do not exist, no option
letters in explanations), and one loads the whole app in a browser and confirms
every pack returns a full, well-formed, explained round.

That catches a great deal. It does not make the content beyond question. If a child
hits an item that seems wrong, it may well be wrong.

October 2026 added more checks: every math generator was run thousands of times and
each question checked for a right key, plausible wrong choices, grade-appropriate
numbers and methods, and what the voice actually says (57 problems found and fixed);
every coaching step was checked for length, symbols the voice cannot say, and answers
given away, and samples were read beside their questions by AI reviewers; and every
Reading Road story was checked for decodability by spelling and by sound. The checks
now live in `tools/` (see [tools/README.md](tools/README.md)), and one of them,
`coach.js`, confirms that every written question carries its own SHOW ME HOW steps.
