# Ticali Trio — Family Edition

A practice game a parent made for three children at Anne Arundel County Public
Schools (AACPS), Maryland: a kindergartener, a 3rd grader and a 5th grader. It is a
family project, not an AACPS product, and it has not been reviewed or endorsed by
AACPS or its teachers. It practices this year's grade-level work in the programs
AACPS uses (Amplify CKLA for reading and Reveal Math for math), explains why every
answer is right, and shows those programs' methods when a child is stuck.

It is one HTML file. On a laptop or desktop it needs no install, no server, no
account and no internet connection, and it has no ads or purchases. Progress is
saved in the browser on the device it is played on.

    Ticali_Trio_Family_Edition.html

What is in it: 237 packs from kindergarten to 5th grade (counting the
kindergartener's pre-K review and each child's puzzle mix); 10,400+ written
questions, each with its own explanation and SHOW ME HOW steps; 780 thinking
puzzles; 11 math packs that make fresh questions every round; and a Reading Road of
40 stops in 11 lands.

On the title screen, just above the three profile cards, is **ℹ️ For grown-ups:
about this game**. It is a page written for teachers and principals: what
the game is and what it is for, what it follows, how it was made and its limits,
privacy, how to try it, and every grade's packs.

For the school, `for-school/` holds a two-page executive summary (Word and PDF) and
a short PowerPoint brief. Replace "[Your name]" in both, then export the PDF again
from the Word file.

## Opening it

- **Laptop or desktop:** save the file (for example, the email attachment) to the
  computer, then double-click it. It opens in the web browser.
- **iPad:** a downloaded copy, from an email or the Files app, opens in a preview,
  and that preview does not run the game. On an iPad the game has to be opened from
  a web address (for example a private link or a family web server). Once it is
  open in Safari from a web address, **Share ▸ Add to Home Screen** gives it an app
  tile that runs full screen.
- **From GitHub Pages:** with this repository public and Pages turned on (Settings ▸
  Pages ▸ Deploy from a branch, the folder `/ (root)`), the game is at
  `https://nicholasticali.github.io/Ticali-trio-education-game/`. `index.html` opens
  the game file. Safari can clear a website's saved data after about a week without
  a visit; a Home Screen app keeps it, so add the game to the Home Screen.

Progress lives in that browser on that device, for that web address. A copy opened
somewhere else starts fresh and never touches the children's progress.

## A round

A pack is a round of ten questions on one topic. It opens with a briefing card that
says why the topic matters. Then, for every question:

- after any answer, right or wrong, **HERE'S WHY** explains it;
- after a wrong answer, **THINK AGAIN** gives a hint and one more try (not on
  true/false, where a second try is a coin flip, or on two-part questions; a parent
  can turn second tries off for test-style practice);
- **💡 SHOW ME HOW** walks through the method step by step, the way the programs
  teach it, with the question's own numbers and words (make a ten, the area model,
  arm blending, look back in the story), then hands the question back. Every one of
  the 10,400+ written questions has its own steps, and math questions made fresh
  each round build theirs from their own numbers.

Question types: multiple choice, true/false, type the answer (with an on-screen
number pad for math), fill in the blank, show your work on paper, and four puzzle
types similar to the kinds of items on Maryland's new MSTAR tests: **pick every
answer that fits, put in order, sort into groups, and answer-then-prove**. Choices
are mixed on every replay, and each round draws its questions at random from the
pack. Eleven math packs (pre-K counting, and the 2nd and 4th grade review packs for
adding, subtracting, place value, word problems, money, time, fractions, decimals,
multiplying and dividing) make fresh questions every round.

## For children who cannot read yet

In kindergarten and 1st grade packs, and in every pack the kindergartener opens,
every question, every answer with words in it, every puzzle piece, sort group and
story has a 🔊 button. The kindergartener's questions also read themselves aloud by
default; the AUTO button in the quiz bar turns that on or off for any child.

- Answer tiles are numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, the number the voice names ("Choice 1").
  Where the answers are numbers or pictures to count, the tiles have no number, so a
  numeral is never set beside a group to count, and the voice reads the answers
  instead.
- In a "which spelling is right?" question the choices are read letter by letter,
  so "noh", "nu" and "no" do not all sound like "no".
- Pictures are named in words, because device voices skip emoji.
- Letter sounds are read as sounds, never letter names. One limit: when the voice
  sounds out a word for blending ("c... a... t"), it says the short sounds /k/ and
  /t/ as "kuh" and "tuh", because a speech engine cannot say them short. The written
  explanations give a key word instead ("the quick sound at the start of tag"), and
  a grown-up can model the short sounds.

The game reads with the most natural voice the device has: Apple's Premium and
Enhanced voices, Microsoft's Natural voices in Edge, Google's voices in Chrome. An
iPad's default voice sounds robotic, so download a natural one: **Settings ›
Accessibility › Read & Speak** (called Spoken Content on older versions) **› Voices
› English**, then a voice marked **Premium** or **Enhanced**, such as Ava, and reload
the game. The parent dashboard also gives the steps for a Mac, lists every voice on
the device with a ▶ sample, and lets you choose. If an online voice fails (no
network), a built-in voice takes over.

## 📖 Reading Road

A game map from first letter sounds to real comprehension, in the order Amplify
CKLA teaches the sounds. Kindergarten: Sound Safari (listening only), Letter Lagoon,
Blend Bridge, Digraph Den, Double Dock and Magic E Mountain; 1st grade: Owl Woods,
Bossy R Ranch and Switch Station; grades 2-3: Big Word Bay (longer words); grades
4-5: Detective Island (comprehension). Each stop teaches one piece of the code (or
one comprehension skill) with learn cards, then gives a short story (read to the
child in the first five stops; after that, one the child reads with only what has
been taught, checked word by word), then practice games and questions about the
story.
Stars unlock the next stop. The kindergartener starts at the first stop, the 3rd
grader at Big Word Bay and the 5th grader at Detective Island; every earlier stop
stays open.

## Fun, and kind

XP, levels, trophies and streaks; a 🐾 buddy that hatches and grows with the child;
a sticker book where every sticker teaches a true fact; three small daily missions
and a day streak (a missed day costs nothing); goofy boss battles that mix ten of
the child's own packs; ⚡ speed sprints for math facts against the child's own best (a
quick random guess costs time, so tapping fast cannot beat thinking); and a 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦
family team goal the three children reach together each week for a reward a grown-up
sets, with no ranking of siblings. A wrong answer never takes away progress. Reduced
Motion is respected.

## On any device

The same round plays the same way on a laptop with only a mouse, a laptop driven by
the keyboard alone (Tab, Enter, A-D or 1-4 to pick a choice, T or F, digits for a
typed answer, Esc), a touchscreen laptop, and an iPad in either orientation. Tap
targets are at least 44 px (52 px for the in-card 🔊), hover effects only apply to a
real mouse, the number pad never raises the iPad keyboard, and the ✏️ scratch pad
takes an Apple Pencil, a finger or a mouse. A quick double tap never presses what
the next screen puts under the finger. Two tabs open at once keep each other's
rounds, and a device that cannot save says so.

## Privacy

There are no accounts or sign-in. The only names are the three profile labels:
Kindergarten, 3rd Grade, 5th Grade. Progress is saved only in that browser, on that
device, and a child's answers are never sent anywhere. When online, the page loads
its typefaces from Google Fonts. In Edge and Chrome some of the most natural voices
are online voices, which receive the words being read aloud: the game's own words,
plus any name a 3rd or 5th grader types for their buddy (up to 12 letters). A parent
can choose a voice built into the device instead.

## What it follows

Written to the programs AACPS uses, **Amplify CKLA** for reading and writing (the
reading packs follow each grade's CKLA units in the 2nd edition, and phonics follows
the CKLA Skills sequence) and **McGraw Hill Reveal Math** for math, and to
Maryland's standards: the revised **math** standards (adopted July 2025, in effect
this school year), the NGSS-based **science** standards, and the **social studies**
framework, revised in 2025. Maryland's grade 5 science test (MISA) covers grades 3,
4 and 5, so the 5th grader's hub keeps 4th grade science on hand. The puzzles
practice formats like those of MSTAR, which replaces MCAP for grades 3-8 reading and
math from spring 2027. Every pack opens with why its topic matters.

## How it was made, and its limits

The questions, explanations, coaching steps and stories were written by an AI
assistant at the parent's direction, working from official curriculum information
that is publicly available. They were then checked by automated tests and by further
AI review passes that re-solved every question.

The limits:

- This is **not an AACPS product** and has not been reviewed or endorsed by AACPS or
  its teachers. Nor is it a product of Amplify or McGraw Hill, the publishers of
  CKLA and Reveal Math, and they have not reviewed or endorsed it.
- The AI assistant could not open several official websites (AACPS, MSDE, McGraw
  Hill and Amplify) directly while the game was built, so some details rest on
  search results that quote those documents. What the AI assistant did read in full
  were PDF copies on public file-storage links.
- It did not use AACPS pacing guides, teacher editions or other licensed classroom
  materials. Math unit order follows McGraw Hill's national editions; how AACPS maps
  Reveal Math onto Maryland's revised standards has not been published.
- Amplify published a 3rd edition of CKLA for 2025-26 with a different order of
  units. Which edition AACPS classrooms use this year could not be confirmed; the
  reading packs follow the 2nd edition's units.
- AACPS's health scope and sequence was not found. Health packs use general
  elementary health content and leave out family-life topics.
- Read-aloud uses a voice from the device or browser, and it says "kuh" and "tuh"
  when it sounds out a word (see above).
- No question cites a standard code; each is written to the content. A question can
  still be wrong. Corrections are welcome.

**[CURRICULUM-NOTES.md](CURRICULUM-NOTES.md)** says exactly what was verified, from
which sources, and what was not.

## Working on it

The app is plain JavaScript in one `<script>`; screens are drawn by `render(html)`.

- **Packs** are listed per profile in `PACKS.k / .g3 / .g5`:

  ```js
  { id:'g3_thing', title:'The Thing', icon:'🧪', subject:'science',
    tier:'now', desc:'What it covers.', bank:'G3_THING' },
  ```

  `tier` is `now`, `review` (the default), `warmup`, `misa` (the fifth grader's
  science test), `ahead` (the kindergartener's 1st grade look ahead) or `think` (Big
  Thinker puzzles); `boss` and `road` are the hidden packs behind boss battles and
  the Reading Road. `subject` picks the hub section. A bank is a plain array of
  question objects registered in `BANKS`.
- **A question** has `type` (`mc`, `tf`, `typed`, `fib`, `showwork`, `order`,
  `sort`, `multi`, `twopart`), its text and key, a `why`, a `hint` (for the second
  try, so true/false and two-part questions have none), and `how: { name, steps }`
  (its own SHOW ME HOW). For early readers, `say` maps a shown
  choice to what its 🔊 says and `sayText` replaces what the question's 🔊 says.
- **Per-pack text:** `PACK_WHY[packId]` (why it matters, on the briefing card) and
  `PACK_HOW[packId]` (the method SHOW ME HOW uses when a question has no steps of its
  own). Puzzles per pack are in `THINK_ITEMS`.
- **Generated math** comes from the generator functions registered in
  `MATH_GENERATORS` and `NONMC_MATH_GENERATORS`; each builds its why, hint and coach
  steps from its own numbers.
- **Fun modules** (buddy, stickers, boss, missions, speed sprints, family goal, and
  the Reading Road with its data) sit after the `/* ═══ FUN MODULES ═══ */` marker,
  each opening with a `// ─── module: <name> ───` line, and talk to the game only
  through `FUN.on`, `FUN.emit`, `FUN.slot`, `FUN.store` and `funSay`, so a broken
  module can never stop a round. Their styles sit after `/* ═══ FUN MODULE STYLES
  ═══ */`. The Reading Road's content is the `READING_ROAD` constant.

## Checks

The checks are in `tools/`; [tools/README.md](tools/README.md) says what each one
proves. They use Playwright with the Chromium at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium` (set
`CHROMIUM_PATH` if yours lives elsewhere):

    cd tools
    npm install
    ./run-all.sh            # every check, one result line each (or: npm test)

One at a time, everything takes about 80 minutes; `JOBS=3 ./run-all.sh` runs three
at a time, and `./run-all.sh repo`, `extras` or `modules` runs one group. After any
change, run at least `node packcheck.js` and `node smoke.js`. After an engine or
input change, also `screens.js`, `platforms.js`, `saves.js` and `speech.js`; after
changing questions or coaching steps, `coach.js`, and for K and 1st grade
`readaloud.js`; after changing a math generator, `generators.js`. After changing a
fun module, run its own suite in `tools/modules/`; after changing the Reading Road's
content, `node modules/readcheck.js` and `node modules/reading.test.js`. The iPad
runs are Chromium with touch input at iPad screen sizes, not real Safari, so try a
round on a real iPad after a big change.
